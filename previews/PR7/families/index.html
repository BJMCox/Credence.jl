Candidate families

Family descriptors define the measures considered during elicitation and their fitted parameters. Load Distributions to use the built-in adapters. Each family accepts starts for initial parameter values. See Named inputs for associating families with parameter references and beliefs.

Families by parameter type

The table suggests candidate families to compare. Elicited beliefs determine their parameters, while the scientific model determines support and any physical bounds.

Example parameterRecommended candidate familiesChoice to examine
Event rate or positive intensityGammaFamily, LogNormalFamilyMass near zero and the upper tail
Probability, efficiency, or proportionScaledBetaFamily(0.0, 1.0)Concentration near either boundary
Time to an event or a characteristic lifetimeGammaFamily, WeibullFamily, LogNormalFamilySkewness and plausible long times
Noise standard deviation or group-level scaleHalfNormalFamily, HalfStudentTFamilyAllowance for large scales
Signed effect, offset, or locationNormalFamily, StudentTFamilyAllowance for large deviations

Set scales in the parameter's physical units and compare the implied prior predictions. Student-t families provide heavier tails than their Normal counterparts. Physical bounds describe possible values; quantile beliefs describe uncertainty within that support. The prior choice guidance discusses scale, tails, and the distinction between physical bounds and prior uncertainty.

Scalar families

The table lists each family's support and parameter keywords. A numeric keyword fixes that parameter. A keyword set to nothing, the default, leaves the parameter free for fitting.

FamilySupportParameter keywords
NormalFamilyReal linelocation, scale
LogNormalFamilyPositive real linelocation, scale on the log scale
GammaFamilyNonnegative real lineshape, scale
WeibullFamilyNonnegative real lineshape, scale
ExponentialFamilyNonnegative real linescale
StudentTFamilyReal linedf, location, scale
CauchyFamilyReal linelocation, scale
LaplaceFamilyReal linelocation, scale
HalfNormalFamilyNonnegative real linescale
HalfStudentTFamilyNonnegative real linedf, scale
ScaledBetaFamily(lower, upper)Interval from lower to upperFitted Beta shapes; fixed bounds

Normal scale is the standard deviation. LogNormal location and scale are the mean and standard deviation of the logarithm. Gamma uses shape and scale, with mean shape * scale. Exponential scale is its mean. Weibull uses shape and scale, with cumulative probability 1 - exp(-(x / scale)^shape) for nonnegative x. Laplace scale gives standard deviation sqrt(2) * scale.

Student-t uses degrees of freedom df and a location-scale transformation of the standard Student-t law. Its mean exists for df > 1, and its variance is scale^2 * df / (df - 2) for df > 2. Cauchy quantiles remain defined despite its undefined mean and variance. Half-Normal and half-Student-t are the corresponding zero-location laws, normalized over nonnegative values. Their scale is the parent law's scale. Quantiles and event probabilities can express beliefs when moments are undefined.

ScaledBetaFamily(lower, upper) maps a Beta law on the unit interval to the specified interval. Its two shape parameters are fitted. Use ScaledBetaFamily(0.0, 1.0) for probabilities and proportions.

Vector families

The dimension n is fixed. The table distinguishes fitted parameters from optional fixed parameter keywords.

FamilySupportFitted parameters and fixed options
MvNormalFamily(n)Real vectors of length nMean and full covariance
MvLogNormalFamily(n)Positive vectors of length nMean and full covariance of the logarithms
MvStudentTFamily(n)Real vectors of length nLocation, scatter, and df; optional fixed df
DirichletFamily(n)Positive vectors of length n summing to oneConcentrations; optional fixed mean, concentration

Multivariate Student-t uses a scatter matrix. Its covariance is df / (df - 2) times that matrix for df > 2. Its mean exists for df > 1. Multivariate LogNormal covariance parameters describe the logarithms rather than the original variables.

DirichletFamily(n) fits all concentration parameters. Fixing mean fits only the total concentration. Fixing concentration fits the mean on the simplex. Supplying both defines a fixed law. The mean must be a positive vector summing to one, and concentration must be positive.

Indexed component beliefs use analytic marginal laws: Normal, LogNormal, Student-t, and Beta for the four vector families above. Vector subsets preserve their original component values and dependence. In particular, a Dirichlet subset retains its original scale and negative covariances. Vector quantile beliefs refer to componentwise marginal quantiles.

Compound priors

Compound families retain coefficients and latent scales in a joint measure. A draw is a named record with a coefficients field and the auxiliary fields listed below. The joint measure preserves the hierarchy's dependence structure. Fixed hyperparameters describe the whole prior. fit estimates the hyperparameters set to nothing.

For sparse effects, compare regularized horseshoe, R2D2, and Dirichlet–Laplace priors. Group horseshoe adds shared shrinkage for related coefficient blocks. Functional horseshoe expresses departures from a simpler function. Multiplicative Gamma shrinkage controls the contribution of successive columns of a loading matrix.

Bind a compound family to a root parameter, such as :theta, and place coefficient beliefs on @vn(theta.coefficients) or an indexed reference. This problem calibrates the global scale of a regularized horseshoe using a probability judgment:

using Credence, MeasureBase, VarNames, Optimization, OptimizationOptimJL, Randomimport Distributionsproblem = Elicitation(    (        @vn(theta.coefficients[1]) =>            (Approximate(:small_effect, Probability(-0.5, 0.5), 0.8; scale=0.1),),    ),    (:theta => (:regularized => RegularizedHorseshoeFamily(3; slab_scale=2.0),),),)report = fit(Xoshiro(501), problem, NelderMead(); sampling=MonteCarlo(1024), maxiters=200)validation = validate(Xoshiro(502), report; sampling=MonteCarlo(8192))selection = select(    report,    only(report.results).id;    validation,    rationale="Reviewed the small-effect probability and slab scale.",)prior = measure(selection)rand(Xoshiro(503), prior)
(global_scale = 1.1571376045079758, local_scales = [2.477962592095722, 1.1266285859204372, 13.467263252925864], slab_aux = 0.5154542641801098, coefficients = [0.6807923506297686, 0.14313269890586625, 0.9468048621749561])

All coefficients share the global scale, but each has a local scale. This probability judgment calibrates a marginal coefficient distribution. Beliefs about the number of effects above a scientific threshold can use Derived. Predictive beliefs can instead calibrate the prior through an observation model. Monte Carlo validation reports fresh uncertainty for these summaries.

The joint measure retains the auxiliary variables needed to evaluate its density. Wren models can draw theta ~ prior and use theta.coefficients in their likelihood. Plaice provides coordinates for the independent base draws. This gives a noncentred parameterization while preserving the joint density and its Jacobian.

Parameter conventions

GroupHorseshoeFamily(groups) takes one group label per coefficient. Labels can repeat, and groups can have different sizes. Even a singleton group has both a group scale and a coefficient-specific scale.

R2D2Family(n; noise_scale, predictor_scales) requires a fixed residual standard deviation and the standard deviations of centered predictors. The Beta law has mean mean_r2 and total shape precision_r2. Its r2 is the conditional prior variance budget, averaged over coefficients and predictors. The realized explained variance of a coefficient draw also depends on the predictor covariance. The constructor implements the Gaussian linear-regression convention with Laplace conditional coefficients.

DirichletLaplaceFamily(n; concentration) uses per-component Dirichlet concentration a = concentration and a total scale with Gamma shape n*a and scale 2. Integrating both auxiliary variables gives independent Gamma-mixture Laplace coefficients. NormalGammaFamily uses Gamma variances with shape shape and scale 2scale^2. At shape=1, its marginal coefficient law is Laplace with scale scale.

FunctionalHorseshoeFamily(design, baseline; baseline_scale) shrinks design * coefficients towards the column space of baseline. The design has full column rank and contains that baseline subspace. baseline_scale supplies a proper Gaussian prior along the baseline directions, in observation-space units. The prior uses the functional horseshoe law on the remaining directions. Design, baseline, and cached geometry arrays are read-only.

MultiplicativeGammaFamily(rows, columns) produces a coefficient matrix of that shape. The local precision has Gamma shape and rate local_df/2. The first precision increment has shape first_shape; later increments have shape later_shape. All increments have unit rate. Expected loading variances decrease across columns when local_df > 2, first_shape > 1, and later_shape > 2. Individual draws can have increases.

Moments and heavy tails

Quantiles and event probabilities remain useful when moments diverge. Direct coefficient moment checks use the conditions below. Dispersion and covariance checks require finite second absolute moments. Auxiliary moment checks use their analytic marginal laws.

Functional horseshoe baseline directions have Gaussian moments. The multiplicative Gamma conditions on later_shape apply after the first column. Monte Carlo evaluation preserves these checks for direct references, including references inside independent combinations and iid arrays. For custom Derived functions and simulators, the user must establish that the requested population moments exist.

Numerical precision

Small Dirichlet concentrations place substantial mass near simplex boundaries. At large dimensions, some allocations can fall below the range of Float64. Higher-precision hyperparameters and a matching draw context preserve those small values:

using Credence, MeasureBase, Randomusing HeterogeneousComputing: GenContextimport Distributionssetprecision(BigFloat, 256) do    n = 256    family = DirichletLaplaceFamily(n; concentration=BigFloat(1)/n, scale=BigFloat(1))    problem = Elicitation((), (:theta => (:dl => family,),))    prior = only(fit(Xoshiro(510), problem).results).measure    draw = rand(GenContext{BigFloat}(Xoshiro(511)), prior)    (minimum_allocation=minimum(draw.allocation), logdensity=logdensityof(prior, draw))end

The context controls draw precision. Matching family hyperparameters also preserves precision in the underlying Gamma and Dirichlet samplers. Use positive, representable allocations when converting a draw to unconstrained coordinates.

The constructions follow the regularized horseshoe, group horseshoe, R2D2, Dirichlet–Laplace, Normal–Gamma, generalized double Pareto, functional horseshoe, and multiplicative Gamma papers.

For truncation, base accepts a scalar family descriptor or a supported Distributions constructor, such as Distributions.LogNormal. Beliefs apply to the resulting truncated law. For an iid array, pass an integer for a vector length or a tuple for its shape. Separate parameter references fit separate laws when entries need different parameters.

Starting coordinates

Supply a named record for one start or a tuple of records for several starts. Scalar families use location directly and log coordinates for each free positive parameter: log_scale, log_shape, or log_df. A fixed parameter has no starting coordinate. For ScaledBetaFamily, use log_alpha and log_beta for the Beta shapes.

Vector Normal, LogNormal, and Student-t families use location, log_scales, and lower. These describe a lower triangular factor L: exp.(log_scales) forms its diagonal, and lower fills its strict lower triangle column by column. The covariance is L * L' for Normal, the log covariance for LogNormal, and the scatter matrix for Student-t. A fitted Student-t df adds log_df.

Dirichlet coordinates depend on its fixed parameters:

FlatPPL export

Scalar families and Dirichlet support FlatPPL export. Gamma and Exponential scales convert to FlatPPL rates. Normal, LogNormal, and supported truncation and iid wrappers preserve their measures. Multivariate Normal and LogNormal exports preserve their full dependence. Multivariate Student-t export requires a corresponding FlatPPL primitive. Compound priors export as jointchain measures with their auxiliary variables and conditional coefficient laws. All nine joint densities match Julia in the FlatPPL JavaScript engine. That engine currently samples the horseshoe, regularized horseshoe, group horseshoe, Normal–Gamma, and generalized double Pareto exports. Its jointchain sampling path still needs support for Dirichlet allocations in R2D2 and Dirichlet–Laplace, conditional multivariate Normal laws in functional horseshoe, and matrix shapes in multiplicative Gamma. See the FlatPPL example for a complete model and density check.
